


<ol type="1">
	<li><em><strong>Yama</strong></em>&nbsp;(moral conduct): noninjury to others, truthfulness, nonstealing, continence, and noncovetousness.</li>
	<li><em><strong></strong></em><em><strong>Niyama</strong></em>&nbsp;(religious observances): purity of body and mind, contentment in all circumstances, self-discipline, self-study (contemplation), and devotion to God and guru.</li>
	<li><strong><em></em><em>Asana</em>:</strong>&nbsp;right posture.</li>
	<li><strong><em>Pranayama</em>:</strong>&nbsp;control of&nbsp;<em>prana</em>, the subtle life currents in the body.</li>
	<li><strong><em>Pratyahara</em>:</strong>&nbsp;interiorization through withdrawal of the senses from external objects.</li>
	<li><strong><em>Dharana</em>:</strong>&nbsp;focused concentration; holding the mind to one thought or object.</li>
	<li><strong><em>Dhyana</em>:</strong>&nbsp;meditation,&nbsp;absorption in the vast perception of God in one of His infinite aspects&nbsp; - Bliss, Peace, Cosmic Light, Cosmic Sound, Love, Wisdom, etc.&nbsp; - all-pervading throughout the whole universe.</li>
	<li><strong><em>Samadhi</em>:</strong>&nbsp;superconscious experience of the oneness of the individualized soul with Cosmic Spirit.</li>
</ol>
