My Sunday pick for you: <em>Allow them to be!</em>

I was 12 when we had this blue Sumo which I would climb to pick berries from the Nellika tree in our compound. It was usually just me and the tree, sometimes the directing voice of my sister and some wandering thoughts.

We had a tiny box for a T.V with 8 buttons on it to switch channels. Couple of hours of Jungle book on Sunday Doordarshan was all we ever used it for.

I remember sitting idly on many summer days just staring out of the window or chatting with my grandma about the day, listening to stories. Mysteries lurked around corners, stones on the streets turned into precious gems and every other object around was stimulation for us to conjure ingenious ideas for creative free-play.

A lot of my writing began around the age of 14 as a result of aimlessly lurking about, inspiration from the outdoors and most definitely the lack of gadgets around me.

The deal: A client recently called me to design a program for her 1.5 year old daughter to enhance skills but mainly something to 'constructively' pass time.

What is the new age parents' obsession with keeping their child occupied and why are we as a society glorifying 'Busy' ? - tuitions, coaching, sports, art, dance, organised play and activities for 'personality' development and then T.V and gadgets and when they have some time after school.

What about stopping
To think
To breathe
To make a new friend or perhaps someone's day?

This silly rat race you've got them part of: asking them what marks they scored, their rank amongst the others, if the won something? 

What about if they saw the pain in someone and stopped to help - or if the day had any love and light and laughs - obviously we forget to ask that! Those are just things that will come along, right? Maybe later.

There is an entire universe within us waiting to be explored.

Take them within.
Allow their minds to wander and be filled with wonder. For boredom to give birth to creativity.
Teach them The joy of nothingness.
The beauty of Here and Now.

The love for simplicity.

We keep asking our kids what they want to become in life. Oh my dear, when all they have to do is just BE! :)
