Bhishma Pitamah's formula for Overcoming  Depression

This is a Great  Story (from the Mahabharata) for Overcoming Depression after any tragedy in Life ... 

After the war had ended and Yudhisthira was crowned as King, Yudhisthira lost his interest in administrative work of the Kingdom. People would come to his Court with their problems, but King Yudhisthira would keep on discussing and lamenting the loss of so many of his kith and kin in war.

He would start telling the visitors about his grief and would say that it was a worthless war that he had fought and he had lost all his near and dear ones. King Yudhisthira was in so much depression, that he would postpone the required action on administrative issues.

This way things started deteriorating due to the inaction on the part of the new King. Since he was King, nobody dared tell Yudhisthira about this bad habit that he had acquired after becoming King.

So the other Pandava brothers and their kin decided to complain about this to Lord Krishna when he was scheduled to visit. Lord Krishna was told about the procrastination habit and the fixation of Yudhisthira on war events and related deaths.

When Lord Krishna asked Yudhisthira about this bad habit, King Yudhisthira replied "O Lord! I have no intention to skip my kingly responsibilities but what should I do? Whenever I want to do my routine work, I get the memories of war and the destruction it caused, so I am unable to concentrate on my current work."  

Lord Krishna decided to solve this problem with the help of an elder and the most senior elder available at that time was Bhishma Pitamah waiting for death on his bed of arrows. So, Lord Krishna went to him alone and told the whole story to him. Bhishma asked lord Krishna to come with Yudhisthira the next evening.

Next evening, Lord Krishna arrived with King Yudhisthira to meet Bhishma Pitamah but Yudhisthira was not aware that Pitamah knew his current problem. So, in the ordinary course of discussions, Pitamah raised the point that he had heard from the public that Yudhisthira was not taking interest in his kingly duties.

Yudhisthira gave the same reply which he had given to Lord Krishna and Pitamah also agreed with his reasoning. Then Pitamah told Yudhisthira that he should declare to the whole world that he had become a King and accomplished something. For this he should organise a great Get-Together function, where he should invite other friendly kings and acquaintances. Yudhisthira agreed to this wish of Bhishma and promised to organise it on a grand scale very soon.

After returning from that meeting, Yudhisthira called his brothers and officers and ordered them to start preparing for a Rajsuya Yagya as Emperors used to perform in those days. An auspicious day after one month was chosen for the Yagya. A lot of preparation and planning started and people started working to make the event memorable.   

After one month, on the scheduled day, the festival happened. Many kings and old acquaintances of the Pandavas and Lord Krishna gathered, had talks, discussions, enjoyed the song and dance programs and participated in a grand feast. The event ended successfully.

After the festival ended, Lord Krishna told Yudhisthira that they should go to meet Pitamah and tell him that his wish had been fulfilled. They went to meet Pitamah. After a few minutes of courtesy talk, Pitamah asked Lord Krishna "Does the general public still complain about the King's loss of interest in governance?"

Lord Krishna replied "No Pitamah! On the contrary, they are now praising the King's fast decision-making and quick action in matters of governance."

Yudhisthira was at a loss to understand the whole conversation, so he asked Pitamah what they were discussing about him. Then Bhishma explained the whole issue to Yudhisthira, which is relevant to all of us, all the time in each human being's life.

He said "O dear grand son! Lord Krishna had told me about your disinterest in governance of the Kingdom and I confirmed it with you also. I could also have advised you in words to stop thinking about past tragedies, but it would not have  worked, just as you ignored Lord Krishna's advice.

Remember Yudhisthira, we are more destroyed by thinking about the miseries of past events than the actual events could cause damage when they happened. The Mahabharta war ended after 18 days, but that war was still continuing in your mind's memory and preventing you from discharging your responsibilities.

This can continue for an indefinite period until another good or bad event takes place. Our mind has the habit to keep alive the memory of the latest event. Therefore, a memory of a bad event just cannot be wished away, but it will be automatically suppressed when another big event takes place.

Now it is not certain when the next big event will happen and it is also not certain that the next event will generate only good memories.

Therefore, the only way out is to plan a good event yourself and get involved in that real and physical event. That is why I asked you to conduct a grand event of Rajsuya Yagya. Once you got a new real project, your mind had to leave the imaginary bad memories of the past because the mind can only do one task at a time.

Only a  lonely person, with a lot of time, resources and no risk of losing his livelihood or life has the luxury of observing melancholy and sorrow for departed souls and that is what you were doing.

Once you get real issues in life, which are  more important and directly affecting you, you will get out of such inaction. So, making the event successful became your real issue and your mind got away from the bad memories. Further, each new action of preparation will generate new good memories and those will suppress all bad old memories .

Finally, the festival day or peak event will certainly generate new memories because of meeting with old acquaintances and participating in happy events, these memories will put the last nail in the coffin of the old bad memories. By  completing the  project successfully, your confidence level will also be boosted."

Yudhisthira profusely thanked Bhishma Pitamah for his great insight and for getting him out of depression and again putting him in his normal mood.

This story is relevant to all of us in life. We all have deaths in our family and each death may be very traumatic for some near relative like son, mother, father, brother, husband, wife, etc.

Sometimes the intensity of death may affect the mind of a really affected relative and such persons may commit suicide also. Because of all these possibilities of depression, we have many physical rituals starting just after a death. We are not sure how these rituals affect the departed soul, but Bhishma's story tells the real importance of such acts and these do help the living family members.

For Hindus, the family where the death happens immediately get into intense action by following many rituals and the peak happens after 10 or 13 days, when a feast is organised for all distant and near relatives. The preparation part is no less than preparation for a marriage .

People may not come for a marriage but they do come for such events. They meet each other, talk about many worldly and esoteric things and this way the family members get more new and better memories which suppress the bad, death memories. 
 
There is a also great astrological value in this story and the after death rituals and feast conducted by Hindus. 

In astrology, Saturn is considered the minister of miseries and worries and death is the  ultimate of all such miseries. So, the death event marks the maximum bad effect of Saturn, the planet for darkness and dukha. And it is not necessary that bad time will create only one death. The intense bad time can cause multiple death events also and many times it  happens also in a very short period.

So Hindu rituals include shaving all hair (Saturn's black or dark significance) by males (represented by Mars, the planet of masculinity and arch enemy of Saturn) and thus reducing probabilty of such misfortune.

Jupiter is the planet with remedial characteristics to counter Saturn's actions. Worship, Puja and donating food and other things fall under Jupiter's domain and that counters  the ill effects of Saturn. People coming for the feast have better Jupiter's protective effect than the family of the departed person at that time, so their gathering at one place effectively counters the ill effects of Saturn by aggregate Jupiter effect like a crowd contributing to a penniless person.

After about 2 weeks from the death event, Saturn would have moved away from the worst position and so life can start again in a positive direction and the aggrieved family members's senses also will have become normal by then.

Though this example is about extreme sorrow called death, it equally applies to any failure in life and consequent depression.

To get away from that, you have to just start a new real, physical  project instead of focussing on failure and past events all the time. It need not be a costly or big project but it should have adequate physical action. 

In one such case of a wealthy lady, she was advised to spend some hours in a crowded market place or travel in a crowded local train for a month and it worked.

Sitting alone with all facilities at one's disposal is the best invitation for continuing with Saturn's gift of depression but it leaves the moment a person starts acting.
