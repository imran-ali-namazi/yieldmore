My Brief Love Affair

The cigarette is burnt out. I’m lounging about with the sacred silences of my living room and that sweet smell of midnight air. It’s only a matter of moments before a very familiar sound in the background punctuates the silence. The rhythmic melody of a train against it’s track, and suddenly I’m transported to a memory. Not of a place, nor a person. But a time. That special time. Summer time.

It was a time when the faint smell of mangoes always lingered around our kitchen. Somehow, whichever time of the day it was, I always found my mother standing there. Telling us off for all the dirt that we dragged in, or the pair of socks we didn’t put to wash. But mostly standing there, with love in her voice, that magically healed our bruised knees and scraped elbows.

It was a time of mornings at the beach making sandcastles, climbing trees, Tom & Jerry marathons. And above all, it was the time for trains. Summer time summer love. You could see the glowing warmth of it splashed across our faces. Waterbottels and packed lunches clutched in our hands, my sister and I would always race each other towards the glorious victory of the window seat. Happiness came in the shape of a square and smelled quite like metal. Journeys those days were a lot longer and carried more meaning than just a destination. They came along with licked-out snack boxes, half tea cups and our eager faces smudged with dust and stories.

We would draw the curtains of our compartment and leap like cats onto our father’s lap, ready for all the tales he wrapped along with him for the journey. We would sit hanging on to every word he uttered – tales of monsters in the dark, the wrath of gods, of lands afar and of the fairies that came to our beds to put us to sleep. Words. Mere words. With the power to incite fear, joy, melancholy and relief – all at the same time.

When dark would fall, Faint outlines of my face reflected by the dim night lights would sit etched on the window glass. I’d lay in deep musing. Often wondering how the moon knew which way I was headed. It always followed me!

There was sometimes a distant sound of a radio pulling me into it’s mellow lull, a stranger’s ears pressed hard against it. Train cutting through the scarlet skies, I especially loved when we were passing by a town. Each window spoke to me, embellished with little star-like dots of light. Of a family at dinner, a girl crying brokenhearted, granny doling out bed-time tales, Lovers in arms. How marvelous.

Every station had a story, and sometimes, just a silhouette of a man.

It was about the journey. And never the destination. My brief love affair.
